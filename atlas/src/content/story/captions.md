The captions of the story's figures. Everything above the first heading is
this note to you and never appears on the page.

HOW IT WORKS. One block per figure. The heading is the figure's number —
the same number you write in the text, as `[FIGURE 05: Press conference]` —
and everything under it, until the next heading, is that figure's caption.
Write as much as you like: blank lines make new paragraphs.

A figure whose images the reader switches with the arrow takes one block per
image: `## 2a` is the first image, `## 2b` the second. Write only `## 2` and
both images share that caption.

You can use `[the words](https://example.com)` for a link, `*italic*` and
`**bold**`. Nothing else — no HTML, ever; the page adds «Figure NN _ » in
front of the caption itself.

The number in the heading is YOUR number, the one in the text. The page
prints it seventeen higher, because the eighteen images of the opening grid
are figures 1 to 18: your `[FIGURE 02]` prints as «Figure 19», your
`[FIGURE 13]` as «Figure 30». Each heading below says which is which.

Delete a block and that figure falls back to the short name inside its
marker in the text.
